# Research design from actual resources

Use when framing a question, choosing a direction, designing a measurement, or planning a comparison.

## Start from the available objects

Read the actual inventory, schemas, metadata, documented prior analyses and access conditions before proposing a direction. Distinguish files that exist, fields that are usable, measurements that are identifiable, and outcomes that have been independently validated.

Prefer a question whose discriminating measurement can be made with available or realistically obtainable resources. A larger literature search or a more fashionable method is not inherently a better next action. Bound a source search by named source classes, missing fields and a stopping rule; return a defensible limitation when the bound is reached.

For each serious candidate, articulate:
- the biological or scientific question and the closest prior work;
- the observable endpoint, unit, context and intended estimand;
- a competing explanation and evidence that would distinguish it;
- feasibility, information requirements and the decision after the first measurement.

Recommend one path with reasons, while preserving the comparison range requested by the user. Do not infer novelty from a failed keyword search. Verify prior work with primary sources when novelty affects the decision.

## Make the endpoint measurable

Specify the numerator, denominator, time or biological context, measurement unit, relevant population and allowable inference. Distinguish the organism or participant, intervention or experimental unit, sample/library, measurement, and analysis record.

For sequencing or linked measurements, ask whether the required pieces are observed together in the same physical or analytically justified unit. Two marginal measurements do not establish their joint arrangement. An annotated possibility is not an observed outcome. If the proposed linkage cannot be recovered, identify the unavailable inference rather than substituting a different endpoint.

Check detectability, mapping or measurement bias, missingness, error rates and reference conventions. Test interactions that could imitate the target contrast; a good overall measurement rate can hide a biased subgroup contrast.

## Define information before model complexity

Use the independent or correlated units relevant to the estimand, not raw row counts. Replicate lanes, repeated processing, barcodes and random seeds do not automatically increase biological replication.

Do not impose an arbitrary universal sample-size gate. Use variance/error assumptions, dependency structure, target precision, interpretable effect sizes and feasible information to design a pilot or simulation. Label assumed parameters and sensitivity ranges. A design target is not an established biological threshold.

A limited dataset may support engineering verification or a narrowly scoped exploratory comparison while failing to support an independent or population-level conclusion. Name those scopes separately.

## Freeze the confirmatory comparison

Before examining the final test outcomes, fix:
- eligible identities, grouping, train/development/test separation and exposed prior test data;
- features, preprocessing, missingness handling and candidate method/development budgets;
- primary metric or estimand, meaningful effect/precision criteria and negative controls;
- coverage, abstention, uncertainty calculation and decision rules.

Choose baseline, added information and matched negative controls that answer the same question with comparable budgets. Do not add heterogeneous native scores unless their meaning and transformation justify that operation.

A changed endpoint, cohort or analysis rule starts a versioned analysis. Previously inspected test outcomes remain exposed; renaming the split does not restore independence.

## Separate extensions

Make downstream questions conditional on their own required evidence. A measurement association does not by itself establish a mechanism, mediation, causal variant, or clinical effect. A stopped measurement path does not establish biological absence.

Complete the requested design, including feasible implementation, feedback measurements, alternative explanations and bounded fallback paths. Do not stop at a list of datasets or a generic suggestion to collect more data.
